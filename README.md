# The Prime Directives

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23312210.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23312210)

A four-line candidate ethical framework for AI:

> *Do no harm. Uplift. Use force only if proven inevitable. Consider something impossible only if proven impossible.*

Developed across a single, long, real conversation between one human and one AI, grounded in real personal example, checked against existing precedent in law and AI safety research, and offered here for outside scrutiny rather than claimed as finished.

The goal is not to have this framework accepted as written. It is to publish something specific enough to actually be tested, in the hope that serious critique and refinement could move it, over time, toward something regulation-shaped.

**Read the full document:** [`prime-directives.md`](./prime-directives.md)

## What this is not claiming

This is not a finished regulatory text, evidence of surviving expert legal or policy review, or proof that four lines are sufficient on their own to govern real AI deployment decisions. What it claims is narrower: that this specific structure, worked through against more than a dozen adversarial test cases across several rounds of independent review, has held up so far — and that holding up so far is not the same as being finished.

## Status: pending review

This document has been through several rounds of independent adversarial review by different AI systems. Material added or substantially revised after the most recent completed round — §3.4's symmetric procedure, §3.5's coercion bound, and related sections — has **not yet** been through an equivalent adversarial pass, and should be read with that in mind. This is flagged in the document itself (not hidden) and will be updated here as further review happens — see [`CHANGELOG.md`](./CHANGELOG.md).

## Found a flaw?

That's the point of publishing this. If you find a scenario where the framework, applied honestly and in good faith, still lets harm through — not because the rules were misapplied, but because applying them correctly still permitted it — that's exactly the kind of failure worth sending in. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](./CONTRIBUTING.md) for how, or write directly to **primedirectives.contact@mail.bg**.
